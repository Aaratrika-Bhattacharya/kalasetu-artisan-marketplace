# Handicraft Marketplace — BTech Startup Project

*Conversation notes, 25 November 2026. Consolidated from the chat; not a verbatim transcript.*

## 1. Original idea (user)

A team of five is working on a startup idea to bring handicrafts and local artisans onto an online platform with national reach. Customers can purchase affordable products through e-commerce, while artisans earn better revenue through wider outreach and reasonable prices.

Observations from artisan surveys:

1. Artifacts are often impulse purchases rather than planned purchases. Generate demand through YouTube advertisements and Instagram reels showing the making process.
2. Handicrafts are generally aesthetic rather than household necessities. Improve conversion by attaching a story to each artifact: making videos, the effort involved, a blog about the maker, the heritage of the art, its origins or historical significance.
3. Many traditional art forms are declining as younger generations stop carrying on the legacy because of low profitability. The mission is to conserve these arts and connect them with interested customers nationally.

## 2. Initial review (assistant)

The concept addresses two problems: limited artisan market access and insufficient customer demand. A conventional e-commerce website alone will not solve the latter.

### Marketing and storytelling

- Use Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Pinterest and festive campaigns to build interest in craftsmanship before selling.
- Each product page can feature artisan background, craft origin, making process, heritage, materials, dimensions, usage and artisan earnings (where appropriate and agreed).
- A QR code on packaging could link to the artisan story.
- Consider curated collections, artisan profiles, workshops and campaigns supporting declining crafts. Verify heritage and endangered-craft claims with reliable sources and communities.

### Challenges

- Balance affordability, fair artisan compensation and platform profitability; avoid competing solely on price with mass-produced goods.
- Plan for limited production capacity using pre-orders, made-to-order products, limited editions and realistic delivery estimates.
- Establish quality checks, protective packaging, shipping and return/damage policies.
- Account for customer acquisition costs; organic content, referrals, creator collaborations and repeat purchases may help.

### Business framework

Discovery and awareness → conversion and commerce → preservation and livelihood.

Position the business as a **discovery-led commerce platform for traditional Indian art**, rather than simply another marketplace.

### Validation and MVP

- Compare product-only listings against story-based listings; measure purchase intent and ultimately actual conversion.
- Test making-process reels, click-throughs and willingness to pay at different prices.
- Interview artisans about earnings, capacity and payment expectations.
- Suggested pilot: 2–3 art forms, 10–15 artisans, 30–50 products, simple website and social campaigns. Track orders, acquisition costs, artisan earnings and feedback. These are illustrative targets, not established commitments.

### Mission and vision

**Vision:** A future where India's traditional art forms thrive, artisans receive recognition and financial value, and customers across the country can access authentic handmade products.

**Mission:** Bridge the gap between local artisans and national consumers through a digital marketplace combining accessibility, fair pricing and storytelling, making traditional handicrafts commercially sustainable and culturally relevant for the next generation.

**Value proposition:** Customers discover meaningful, authentic handmade products; artisans reach a wider market, earn fairly and preserve their artistic legacy.

Limited market reach → low sales and income → younger generations lose interest → decline of traditional crafts. The platform aims to break this cycle. Storytelling must translate into sustainable income and consistent demand.

## 3. User's clarifications

**A. Pricing:** Sell at a reasonable price that allows a platform margin and good artisan earnings. A product priced slightly above its local market price might be expensive locally but affordable in another part of the country where demand exists.

**B. Supply:** Artisans often have another 4–5 families making the same art. As demand increases, the platform can onboard additional families.

**C. Packaging:** Use robust packaging, including bubble wrap and cushioning.

**D. Marketing:** As IIT Kharagpur students, the team can leverage its network for creator collaborations.

## 4. Review of clarifications (assistant)

### A. Regional willingness to pay

Connecting local supply with customers elsewhere who recognize its value is a potential market opportunity. Compare destination-market prices, ensure transparent shipping charges and agree fair artisan compensation.

Illustrative pricing: artisan receives ₹400, platform gross margin ₹100, selling price ₹500 before accounting for packaging, logistics, payments and applicable taxes.

### B. Artisan cluster model

Start with one family, list a few products, and expand to other families as demand grows. Track production capacity, specialization, lead times, quality and raw material availability. Similar craftsmanship does not guarantee identical products or quality. Maintain minimum quality standards while preserving individual uniqueness. Consider confirmed orders before production.

### C. Packaging

| Product | Packaging |
|---|---|
| Terracotta/pottery | Multi-layer cushioning and fitted inserts |
| Paintings | Rigid sheets and corner guards |
| Wooden crafts | Corrugated boxes and protective wrapping |
| Textiles | Moisture-resistant bags and outer packaging |
| Metal crafts | Scratch-resistant wrapping and cushioning |

Test transit durability, cost and weight; consider eco-friendly materials where feasible.

### D. IIT Kharagpur network

Collaborate with photography enthusiasts, video creators, design students, cultural and entrepreneurship clubs, alumni and campus festivals. A campus exhibition of 15–20 products could test attention, willingness to pay, actual sales and the impact of stories.

### Five pillars

1. National market access.
2. Fair and viable pricing.
3. Story-led marketing.
4. Artisan cluster network.
5. Reliable fulfillment.

Build relationships with artisan communities, share customer feedback, develop suitable product variations without compromising traditional methods, and distribute orders fairly.

**Problem:** Lack of market access and financially sustainable demand.

**Solution:** The platform.

**Social impact:** Preservation of traditional art forms.

Validate willingness to buy, unit economics and scalable supply.

### 12-slide pitch deck

1. **Introduction:** Brand, tagline, team, one-line description.
2. **Problem:** Limited reach, visibility, income and intergenerational continuity; actual artisan survey evidence.
3. **Market opportunity:** Impulse/aesthetic purchases, discovery challenges, regional demand differences, customer segments and alternatives.
4. **Solution:** National story-led marketplace and fair pricing.
5. **Customer journey:** Reel → website → story → purchase → packaging/delivery → feedback.
6. **Value proposition:** Benefits for artisans, customers, platform and society.
7. **Supply chain:** Identification, onboarding, listings, orders, quality checks, packaging, shipping, settlement and cluster expansion.
8. **Financial model:** Revenue, pricing, unit economics and expenses.
9. **Marketing:** Reels, Shorts, collaborations, campus networks, exhibitions and festivals.
10. **Launch and growth:** Small pilot, demand testing, operational improvements and expansion.
11. **Social impact:** Earnings, demand, recognition and continuity, with measurable indicators.
12. **Roadmap and conclusion:** Milestones, risks, next steps and mission.

### Supporting report structure

Executive summary; problem identification and survey evidence; market analysis; proposed solution; business model; operations; marketing; financial feasibility; implementation plan; social impact; survey appendix.

## 7. Core pitch takeaway

**A discovery-led e-commerce platform that connects India's traditional artisans with national customers through storytelling, fair pricing and accessible commerce.**

Artisan livelihood is the core business objective, storytelling is the proposed differentiator, and e-commerce is the medium. The commercial and social outcomes remain hypotheses to validate through a pilot and actual survey/order evidence.
